ABSTRACT 
 
Agriculture accounts for a significant portion of global freshwater consumption, making efficient 
water management essential for sustainable farming. Traditional irrigation practices often rely 
on manual observation and estimation of soil conditions, which  can result in overwatering, 
underwatering, reduced crop productivity, and unnecessary water wastage. To address these 
challenges, this project presents Neuro VP Dhara Core (Smart  Roots), an IoT-based intelligent 
soil monitoring and predictive water management system designed for small and medium-scale 
agricultural applications. 
The proposed system utilizes an ESP32 microcontroller as the central processing unit, integrated 
with a Capacitive Soil Moisture Sensor and DHT22 Temperature and Humidity Sensor to 
continuously monitor environmental and soil conditions. Real -time sensor da ta is collected, 
processed, and transmitted to cloud platforms for remote monitoring and analysis. Based on 
predefined moisture thresholds, the system can automatically control a water pump through a 
relay module, ensuring efficient irrigation and reducing human intervention. 
In addition to real -time monitoring, the system incorporates AI -assisted recommendations to 
support informed irrigation decisions and improve overall farm management. The integration of 
IoT technologies, cloud connectivity, and automation enables farmers t o access live data, 
optimize water usage, and enhance agricultural productivity. The proposed solution is designed 
to be affordable, scalable, and suitable for deployment in resource -constrained farming 
environments. 
The project demonstrates how smart sensing, automation, and data -driven decision-making can 
contribute to sustainable agriculture by minimizing water wastage, improving crop health, and 
supporting environmentally responsible farming practices.

CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION 
1.1 Background and Concept 
Agriculture is one of the most water -intensive sectors, consuming nearly 70% of the world's 
freshwater resources. Efficient irrigation management is therefore essential for ensuring 
sustainable agricultural practices and maximizing crop productivity. However, many farmers 
still rely on traditional irrigation methods that depend on manual observation and estimation 
of soil conditions. These practices often result in overwatering or underwatering, leading to 
water wastage, reduced crop yield, and increased operational costs. 
Recent advancements in the Internet of Things (IoT), embedded systems, and artificial 
intelligence have enabled the development of smart agriculture solutions capable of 
monitoring environmental conditions in real time. By integrating sensors, wireless 
communication, cloud platforms, and automated control systems, farmers can make informed 
decisions regarding irrigation and crop management. 
The proposed project, Neuro VP Dhara Core (SmartRoots), is an intelligent soil monitoring 
and predictive water management system that utilizes an ESP32 microcontroller, soil moisture 
sensing, temperature and humidity monitoring, cloud connectivity, and AI -assisted 
recommendations. The system continuously moni tors soil and environmental conditions, 
provides real -time data visualization, and automatically controls irrigation based on soil 
moisture levels. This approach helps optimize water usage, improve crop health, and support 
sustainable farming practices. 
1.2 Need for the Project 
Agricultural productivity is highly dependent on proper irrigation and environmental 
management. Many small and medium -scale farmers lack access to affordable technologies 
that provide real -time information about soil and climatic conditions. As a result, irrigation 
decisions are often based on experience or assumptions rather than actual field data. 
The major needs addressed by this project are: 
• Lack of real-time soil monitoring systems for small-scale farmers. 
• Excessive water wastage due to inefficient irrigation practices. 
• Reduced crop productivity caused by improper soil moisture levels.

1.3 Problem Statement

small-scale farmers. Therefore, there is a need for an affordable, reliable, and intelligent 
monitoring system that can provide real-time soil and environmental data, automate irrigation 
decisions, and support efficient water management practices.

CHAPTER 3 :  METHODOLOGY & DESIGN 
3.1 Block Diagram 
The block diagram illustrates the overall architecture of the Neuro VP Dhara Core 
(SmartRoots) system. The ESP32 microcontroller collects data from the capacitive soil 
moisture sensor and DHT22 sensor, processes the information, and controls the water pump  
through a relay module while transmitting real -time data to the cloud dashboard for 
monitoring and AI-assisted irrigation recommendations. 
 
Figure 3.1 – Block Diagram 
3.2 Working Principle 
The Neuro VP Dhara Core (SmartRoots) system operates by continuously monitoring soil and 
environmental conditions using a Capacitive Soil Moisture Sensor and a DHT22 Temperature 
and Humidity Sensor. These sensors are interfaced with the ESP32 microcontroll er, which acts 
as the central processing unit of the system. The entire prototype is powered through a USB 

--- Page 20 ---
                                                                                                                                                       NEURO VP DHARA CORE 
Dept of EEE & Dept of CSE(IOT)DSCE, 2025-2029                                                                                                                                       9 
connection from a laptop, which also serves as the platform for programming, monitoring, and 
data visualization. 
The soil moisture sensor measures the moisture content present in the soil, while the DHT22 
sensor records the surrounding temperature and humidity. The ESP32 periodically acquires these 
sensor readings and compares the soil moisture value with a predefine d threshold. If the soil 
moisture level falls below the threshold, the ESP32 activates a relay module, which switches ON 
the water pump to irrigate the soil. Once the moisture level reaches the desired range, the relay 
is deactivated, turning the pump OFF and preventing overwatering. 
Simultaneously, the collected sensor data is transmitted through Wi -Fi to cloud platforms such 
as Firebase and HiveMQ for real -time monitoring and storage. The data is displayed on a web 
dashboard, enabling users to remotely observe field conditions. Based on the collected data, the 
AI recommendation module analyzes environmental trends and provides intelligent suggestions 
related to irrigation management and crop care. This integrated approach helps optimize water 
usage, reduce manual intervention, and improve agricultural productivity through smart decision-
making.

CHAPTER 4 : IMPLEMENTATION & RESULTS 
4.1 Implementation Steps 
The implementation of the Neuro VP Dhara Core system was carried out through a systematic 
integration of hardware and software components. The following steps were followed during 
the development process: 
Step 1: Component Selection 
The required hardware components such as ESP32, Capacitive Soil Moisture Sensor, DHT22 
Sensor, 16×2 LCD Display with I2C Module, Relay Module, Water Pump, Breadboard, and 
Jumper Wires were selected based on project requirements. 
Step 2: Circuit Assembly 
All components were interconnected using a breadboard and jumper wires. The sensors, display 
module, relay, and water pump were connected to the ESP32 according to the designed circuit 
configuration. 
Step 3: ESP32 Programming 
The ESP32 was programmed using Arduino IDE. The program was developed to: 
• Read soil moisture values. 
• Measure temperature and humidity using DHT22. 
• Display sensor readings on the LCD. 
• Control the relay and water pump automatically. 
• Transmit sensor data through Wi-Fi for cloud monitoring. 
Step 4: Sensor Calibration 
The soil moisture sensor was calibrated by observing readings under dry and moist soil 
conditions. Threshold values were determined to ensure accurate pump activation and 
deactivation. 
Step 5: LCD Display Integration 
--- Page 23 ---
                                                                                                                                                       NEURO VP DHARA CORE 
Dept of EEE & Dept of CSE(IOT)DSCE, 2025-2029                                                                                                                                       12 
The 16×2 LCD display was interfaced with the ESP32 using the I2C communication protocol. 
Real-time temperature, humidity, and pump status information were displayed continuously. 
Step 6: Relay and Pump Testing 
The relay module was connected to the water pump and tested under different soil moisture 
conditions. The pump was configured to switch ON when moisture levels dropped below the 
predefined threshold and switch OFF when sufficient moisture was detected. 
Step 7: Cloud Connectivity 
The ESP32 was connected to a Wi -Fi network to enable communication with cloud services 
such as Firebase and HiveMQ. Sensor data was uploaded periodically for remote monitoring. 
Step 8: Dashboard Development 
A web-based dashboard was developed using React.js to visualize real -time sensor readings, 
irrigation status, and historical data. 
Step 9: AI Recommendation Integration 
Collected environmental data was processed through the AI recommendation module to 
generate intelligent irrigation suggestions and improve water management decisions. 
Step 10: System Testing and Validation 
The complete system was tested under different environmental conditions. Sensor readings, 
pump response, cloud communication, and dashboard performance were monitored to verify 
proper operation and reliability. 
Step 11: Performance Evaluation 
The system performance was evaluated based on sensor accuracy, response time, automation 
efficiency, and cloud data transmission reliability. The obtained results demonstrated effective 
monitoring and automated irrigation control.

4.6 Limitation 
 
• The system monitors only soil moisture, temperature, and humidity, while other important 
agricultural parameters are not considered. 
• Real-time monitoring and cloud connectivity depend on the availability of a stable Wi-Fi 
connection. 
• Sensor readings are collected from a single location and may not represent the entire field 
accurately. 
• The prototype is powered through a laptop USB connection and is not intended for direct 
standalone field deployment. 
• The irrigation system operates based on predefined moisture threshold values, which may 
need adjustment for different crops and soil conditions.
